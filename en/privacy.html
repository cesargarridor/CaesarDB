Privacy policy

Last updated: 22 November 2026

Summary. CaesarDB has no telemetry. Your credentials and configuration stay on your machine, encrypted, and are not sent to the author. The only network connections are your AWS endpoint, license validation with Polar and update checks on GitHub.

Controller

Cesar Garrido (persona física), tax ID 78164624K, Avenida Madrid, 23003 Jaén (España). Contact: cgarrui0301@gmail.com.

What data the application touches

  • Credentials and configuration (secret access keys, session tokens, proxy credentials, profiles and connections): stored only on your machine, in locally encrypted files (AES-256-GCM, with the key kept in the system keychain). They are not sent to the author or to any server of ours.
  • Your table data: travels only between your machine and your DynamoDB endpoint (AWS, your local emulator, or the proxy you configure). The author does not receive it.
  • Zero telemetry: no analytics, no crash reports, no usage identifiers.
  • Files you export or import are chosen by you and saved where you say.

Network communications by the author or third parties

Besides whatever you configure towards AWS, the application makes only these two communications:

  • License validation with Polar: the license key and the machine name are sent to activate or check it.
  • Update check on GitHub Releases (a manifest and signed packages are downloaded). GitHub may see your IP address as with any download.

Payments and billing: Polar

Purchases are processed by Polar as merchant of record and as an independent processor/controller of payment and billing data (name, email, address, country, taxes). The author does not see your card or bank details and only receives from Polar the information needed to manage your license and customer support. See Polar’s privacy policy on its site. You can manage your activations and receipts in the customer portal.

This website

The site is static: it uses no cookies, includes no analytics, trackers, third-party fonts or scripts, and stores nothing in your browser (see Cookies). The download buttons query GitHub’s public API (api.github.com) to find the latest installer; GitHub may see your IP address in that request. It is hosted on a static hosting provider (GitHub Pages), which may process your IP address in technical server logs under its own policy.
